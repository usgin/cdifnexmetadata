# NXsas — what it is

`NXsas` is the NeXus **application definition** for raw small-angle
scattering data collected with a monochromatic beam and an area
detector.

In the definition's own words, it covers *"raw, monochromatic 2-D SAS
data with an area detector"*, and is meant to suit both **SANS**
(small-angle neutron scattering) and **SAXS** (small-angle X-ray
scattering). It explicitly covers *"SAS, WSAS, grazing incidence and
GISAS"* — that is, plain small-angle scattering, **GISAS**
(grazing-incidence small-angle scattering), and anything else
monochromatic that reads out a 2-D detector.

`WSAS` is the definition's own abbreviation and it is **not expanded
anywhere in the NeXus source**, so it is left unexpanded here rather
than guessed at. The context — listed beside plain SAS, in the way SAXS
is usually paired with WAXS (wide-angle X-ray scattering) — suggests a
wider-angle variant, but that reading is inference, not something the
definition states.

Throughout this document **SAS** means small-angle scattering, the
technique family, without committing to neutrons or X-rays.

Two boundaries matter:

- **Raw, not reduced.** `NXsas` describes what came off the instrument.
  Azimuthally averaged *I(Q)* curves are a different thing and belong to
  `NXcanSAS`.
- **Monochromatic, not time-of-flight.** A wavelength-dispersive SAS
  measurement is `NXsastof`.

## What the measurement is

In a SAS experiment a collimated, monochromatic beam passes through a
sample and most of it goes straight through. A small fraction scatters
through small angles, and that scattering carries information about
structure on length scales far larger than the wavelength — 1–100 nm
typically: polymer chains, protein assemblies, voids, precipitates.

The primary datum is a **2-D image**: scattered intensity as a function
of position on the detector. Position converts to scattering vector *Q*
given three things the definition therefore insists on:

| To compute *Q* you need | `NXsas` field |
|---|---|
| wavelength | `NXmonochromator/wavelength` |
| sample-to-detector distance | `NXdetector/distance` |
| where the direct beam lands | `NXdetector/beam_center_x`, `beam_center_y` |
| pixel size | `NXdetector/x_pixel_size`, `y_pixel_size` |

That is the interesting structural point for CDIF (Cross-Domain
Interoperability Framework): **the intensity array alone is not
interpretable.** Unlike an XAS (X-ray absorption spectroscopy) spectrum,
where energy is stored beside μ(E) as a coordinate array, a SAS image
stores only pixel indices. The coordinate axis has to be *reconstructed* from scalar
instrument geometry. So the geometry scalars are not incidental context
here — they are part of the data description.

## Structure

```
NXentry                     title, start_time, end_time, definition
├── NXinstrument            name
│   ├── NXsource            name, type, probe
│   ├── NXmonochromator     wavelength, wavelength_spread
│   ├── NXcollimator
│   │   └── NXgeometry
│   │       └── NXshape     shape, size          (collimation length)
│   └── NXdetector          data, distance,
│                           x_pixel_size, y_pixel_size,
│                           beam_center_x, beam_center_y,
│                           polar_angle, azimuthal_angle,
│                           rotation_angle, aequatorial_angle
├── NXsample                name, aequatorial_angle
├── NXmonitor               integral, mode, preset
└── NXdata                  (links to the detector data)
```

`NXsas` does not `extend` another application definition — it is
self-contained, unlike the restructured `NXxas` family where
`NXxas_trans extends NXxas`.

## How real files differ from the definition

Both example files in the corpus declare `definition = "NXsas"`, and
neither matches it exactly. This is worth stating plainly because it
drives how the crosswalk is written.

**`autogenerated_examples/.../NXsas.hdf5`** follows the definition
field-for-field, because it was generated from it. Its arrays are all
placeholder scalars.

**`APS/NXsas/hdf5/nexus-example.hdf5`** is a real measurement from
beamline 9-ID-C at the APS (Advanced Photon Source), made on its
**USAXS** (ultra-small-angle X-ray scattering) instrument in its
**pinSAXS** (pinhole SAXS) configuration. It differs from the definition
in ways the definition does not anticipate:

- The detector group has **no `data` field**. The measurement lives in
  `NXdata` as `frames`, shape `(10, 195, 487)` — ten frames of a
  195×487 detector — with `blank` (195, 487) beside it as a background.
- `NXdata` supplies its own axes, `horizontal` (487) and `vertical`
  (195), which the definition does not mention at all.
- `NXmonochromator` carries `energy` as well as `wavelength`.
- `NXsource` carries `current` and `energy`.
- The collimator's `NXshape` uses `size_x`/`size_y`/`center_x`/`center_y`
  rather than the definition's single `size`.

The standards-based crosswalk maps the definition. Where a writer put
something else, that belongs in `legacy-paths.tsv` — which is exactly
the split the Athena/GSECARS case established for XAS, arrived at again
independently here.
